Non-Programmable Checks

Mechanism: blockrand-drand-quicknet

HMAC-SHA256 used No
EvidenceBlockRand derives the final seed as SHA256(player_secret:server_secret:drand_signature). This is bare SHA-256 over a concatenated input, not HMAC-SHA256. Length-extension attacks are theoretically applicable. The risk is low because none of the three inputs are attacker-controlled during the bet, but the construction is not formally secure against this class of attack.

CSPRNG used Yes
Generator namedrand quicknet (BLS12-381 G1 threshold signature)
Approved construction Yes
Evidencedrand produces randomness via a threshold BLS12-381 signature over the round number. This is a well-studied, bias-resistant construction. The League of Entropy nodes are operated by independent institutions. The output is cryptographically unpredictable unless a threshold of nodes collude.

Generated client-side Yes
EvidenceBlockRand's protocol requires the player to supply their own player_secret before the game begins. The server does not generate or suggest this value. Providing a secure player_secret is entirely the player's responsibility.

Evidence category0.5
DescriptionThe server commits SHA256(server_secret) before the player places a bet, and the agreed future drand round acts as an independent temporal anchor (the round number is publicly known and its signature has not been published yet). The server commitment is not on-chain or timestamped by a third party. You are trusting BlockRand's server logs as proof the commitment was made before the client seed was received.

Threat ActorResistedReasoning
Dishonest Operator PASS Assuming the protocol is followed correctly, the operator commits SHA256(server_secret) before receiving the player_secret, and the drand round is agreed in advance. The operator is unable to pick a favourable server_secret after seeing the player's input, as they have already published its hash. The drand output is outside the operator's control.
Malicious Player PASS The player does not know server_secret at bet time. Only its SHA-256 hash is disclosed. Without knowing both server_secret and the future drand signature, the player is unable to predict or reverse-engineer the outcome, even if they choose their player_secret strategically.
External Eavesdropper PASS SHA-256 is a one-way function. Observing the derived seed or dice outcome reveals nothing about the three inputs. The drand signature is public knowledge, but server_secret and player_secret are not.
Compromised Oracle PASS drand uses a t-of-n threshold BLS signature across the League of Entropy. A single compromised node is unable to bias or predict the output. Collusion of t nodes is required. The threshold is documented in the chain's group configuration.
